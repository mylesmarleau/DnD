Ability Scores

These 6 numbers describe your character. The big colored number is your modifier — that's what actually gets added to rolls. When the DM asks for "a Strength check" or similar, hit that ability's Roll Check button to see the modifier, then roll your own d20 and add it.

Saving Throws

A saving throw is a roll to resist or shrug off something happening to you — a trap, a spell, poison. The DM will say "make a [ability] save" and tell you the DC (the number you need to meet or beat). Click Roll next to that ability.

Skills (passive perception: 10)

A skill check is when you try to do something uncertain — sneak (Stealth), persuade someone (Persuasion), notice something (Perception), recall lore (History), etc. The DM tells you what to roll or you suggest one that fits your action. Click Roll next to the matching skill to see your bonus, then roll your own d20 and add it. Higher = better. The two checkboxes are for proficient (you're trained in it) and expertise (double proficiency, rare) — check these based on your character sheet/class.

Combat

Your Armor Class (AC) is how hard you are to hit — an enemy's attack roll (their d20 + their bonus) has to meet or beat it to hit you. You don't roll anything for this; it's just a number the DM compares against. Initiative decides turn order at the start of a fight — leave it blank to auto-use your DEX modifier and just click Roll Initiative in the Combat Tracker below when a fight starts.

Combat Tracker

When a fight starts: everyone rolls initiative (click Roll Initiative below) — the DM calls out names highest to lowest, and you act on your turn in that order, every round, until the fight ends.

On your turn you generally get: one Movement (up to your Speed), one Action (Attack, cast a spell, Dash, Dodge, Help, Hide, etc.), and one Bonus Action only if a feature/spell gives you one. You can do these in any order. Click the tags below to mark what you've used — they'll reset for you when you hit "Next Round". A Reaction can be used on someone else's turn (e.g. an opportunity attack) — you get one per round.

Not sure what to do on your turn? A safe default: move into range, then Attack — pick a weapon/spell in "Attacks & Spellcasting" below and click its Roll button.

Roll Reference

You're using your own physical dice. Click any Roll button on the sheet (ability, save, skill, attack, initiative) and the bonus to add shows up here.
Most rolls in D&D are a d20 plus a modifier, compared against a target number (an enemy's AC, or a DC the DM sets). Advantage means roll two d20s yourself and take the higher (good for you); Disadvantage means take the lower (bad for you) — the DM will tell you when either applies.

Spellcasting

Casting a spell that targets someone uses either an attack roll (d20 + Attack Bonus vs their AC, like a weapon) or forces them to make a saving throw against your Save DC (they roll, trying to meet or beat that number). Each spell says which. Casting a spell of level 1+ uses up one Spell Slot of that level or higher — click a pip below to mark it used, and they all come back on a Long Rest. Cantrips (level 0) don't use slots.

New Player Guide

You don't need to memorize any of this — just glance back here (or turn on Beginner Mode for hints right on the sheet) whenever you're not sure what to do.

The basic loop

The DM describes a situation. You say what your character does. If the outcome is uncertain, you roll dice — usually a d20 plus a modifier — and compare it to a target number. Higher is better. That's really it; everything below is a variation on this.

The three kinds of rolls

  • Ability check — you're trying to do something (climb, sneak, persuade, recall a fact). Roll d20 + the matching skill's bonus (or plain ability modifier if no skill fits). The DM sets the target number (the DC) and tells you if you succeed.
  • Saving throw — something is happening to you (a trap, a spell, poison) and you're trying to resist it. Roll d20 + that ability's save bonus, versus a DC the DM gives you.
  • Attack roll — you're trying to hit something in combat. Roll d20 + your attack bonus; if it meets or beats the target's Armor Class (AC), you hit and roll damage.

In every case: find the matching row on the sheet and click its Roll button to see the bonus — it shows up in the Roll Reference log. Then roll your own d20, add that bonus, and tell the DM the total.

Your turn in combat

  1. When a fight starts, everyone rolls initiative — click Roll Initiative in the Combat Tracker. Turn order goes highest to lowest, every round.
  2. On your turn you can: Move up to your Speed, take one Action, and take one Bonus Action if something grants you one — in any order.
  3. Simplest action: Attack. Pick a weapon/spell in "Attacks & Spellcasting," click Roll for the to-hit roll, and if it beats the target's AC, roll the listed damage dice too.
  4. Other common actions: Cast a Spell, Dash (double your movement), Dodge (attacks against you have disadvantage), Help (give an ally advantage), Hide.
  5. Not your turn? You can still take a Reaction once per round if something triggers one (most common: an opportunity attack when an enemy walks away from you).
  6. Mark what you've used with the Action/Bonus/Reaction tags in the Combat Tracker — they reset automatically when you click "Next Round."

Taking damage & healing

When you're hit, enter the damage amount and click Damage in the Hit Points panel. If you hit 0 HP you fall unconscious and roll Death Saves on your turns until stabilized or healed — flag this to your DM immediately, there are extra rules they'll walk you through. Between fights, a Short Rest lets you spend Hit Dice to heal some HP; a Long Rest heals you fully and resets spell slots and most limited-use features.

Things nobody tells you up front

  • You can describe what you want to do in plain words ("I want to search the desk") — you don't need to know the exact rule name. The DM will tell you what to roll, if anything.
  • It's completely normal to ask "what does that mean?" or "what are my options here?" mid-session.
  • Rolling low isn't just bad — it's often the most interesting outcome at the table. A missed attack or a failed check is a normal part of the story, not something to apologize for.
  • You can always ask the DM "can I use my [feature/spell name] here?" if you're not sure something applies.
